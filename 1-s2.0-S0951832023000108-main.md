| Appendix B. |     | Corrosion model based on an Evans’ diagram  |     |     |     |     |
| ----------- | --- | ------------------------------------------- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
Corrosion is the result of two half-cell reactions, an anodic reaction (oxidation) and a cathodic reaction (reduction), that results from an ionic
current flowing through a concrete pore [62,63]. The logarithmic current is linearly proportional to the corrosion potential, which is illustrated in a
Tafel plot (Fig. B1) [63]. This relationship describes both the reduction and oxidation reactions. As corrosion occurs when the oxygen reduction
reaction and the reinforcement oxidation reaction have the same reaction rate, the corrosion current and the corrosion potential is the point where the
Tafel plots of the reduction reaction and oxidation reaction meet, which is depicted in an Evans’ diagram (Fig. B1) [64].
