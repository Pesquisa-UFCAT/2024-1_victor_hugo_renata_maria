Keywords: Climate change accelerates carbonation in concrete, raising risks of cracking and spalling. However, existing
Maintenance model formulations often oversimplify this impact, inadequately representing the intrinsically non-linear
Markov processes and phase-dependent behavior of the carbonation process. In this paper, we propose a novel framework
Inspection that integrates Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and a Predictive Markov Decision Process with
Multi-state system
Phase-type Approximation (PMDP-PH), enabling resilient and cost-effective infrastructure maintenance under
Degradation
carbonation risks. PINNs embed governing physical laws within neural architectures, enabling accurate
inference of carbonation dynamics even under limited observational data. This framework accommodates non-
exponential sojourn time distributions in both the initiation and propagation phases, effectively approximated
using hypo-exponential models. The PMDP-PH adaptively updates inspection and maintenance strategies
by continuously refining the remaining useful life (RUL) distribution in real time. This decision-making
process is formulated as a tractable multi-stage model predictive control (MPC) problem over selected belief
states, ensuring a non-decreasing value function throughout the robust horizon. Applied to a representative
infrastructure system, our method reduces total maintenance costs by up to 61.9% compared to benchmark
strategies under variable deterioration scenarios. These findings highlight the promise of combining physics-
informed learning with a new form of predictive control strategy to strengthen infrastructure resilience under
the growing threat of carbonation-induced deterioration.
